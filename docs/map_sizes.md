# Map sizes (0.15.0)

The owner asked, on 2026-10-28, first for Greenhold: "Scoot the spots a bit closer to the main and squeeze 2 more spots in this map." Then for every map. Then: enlarge all maps first, then place the base sites on the bigger maps.

## What changed

- Every built-in map is bigger. Most grow by about 1.3 on each side. That is about 1.75 times the ground.
- The classic maps go from 84 x 60 to 110 x 80. The walk from main to main is about 113 tiles. A Musketeer walks it in about 58 seconds.
- The team maps grow by the same factor.
- Some 1v1 maps already had a long walk. They grow a little less: Ashford Crossing by 1.2, Amberfall Vale by 1.21, Thornwood Maze by 1.16, and Saltmarsh Isles, Frostmere and Hollowfen by 1.15.
- Positions grow with the map. Widths do not. Ramps, rivers, fords and tree walls stay as wide as before.
- A main's plateau is one tile bigger each way. A main by the edge of the map keeps its place there, so its starting buildings sit as before. Its ramp and its natural keep their place next to it.
- Lakes and mesas grow a little. Forests grow with the map. The random woods grow with the ground.
- Every map has more base sites. They are the same for every player: the maps are still turned through their middle.
- Each side's natural is close to its main, in front of its ramp. Then come 3 or 4 more sites per side: corners, beside ponds and lakes, and pockets.
- No free site is within 24 steps of another player's main.
- Every site holds a hall's full reserves, for the Crown's farms and powder and the Clockwork's ore. Trees and decorations are cleared off each site and thinned round it.

## Old matches

- A replay of a built-in map from before 0.15.0 says its map was made bigger, and does not play. It was played on the old ground.
- A saved match of a built-in map from before 0.15.0 is not offered to resume, for the same reason.
- Your own maps keep their size. Old ones without a size still load at 84 x 60.
- A new map in the editor starts at 110 x 80. The editor's sizes now go from 64 x 64 to 256 x 256, with presets for every built-in size.

## Performance

The same 2v2 match (Crown, Brood, Crown, Clockwork, Hard computers, seed 4242) was played for 12 game minutes on The Sundered Realm, the biggest map: once at its new size (252 x 210), once at its old size (192 x 160). Both ran on the same machine, one after the other. Results: docs/perf/v0.15_sizes_big_sundered_2v2_pc.md and docs/perf/v0.15_sizes_small_sundered_2v2_pc.md.

- The processor time per game minute is about the same: 37.8 s against 35.7 s in minute 12, and within a few percent in every minute.
- Both games had about 310 units at the end.
- The frame times differ more (40 against 59 frames a second in minute 12, and some very long single frames on both maps). The machine was shared with two other jobs, so the frame times are noisy. The processor time is the fair number.
- The fog, the pathfinder and the unit grid cost a little more on the big map (fog 0.69 against 0.49 ms a frame, the unit grid 0.55 against 0.24). They grow with the ground, not with the units.
- The minimap and the camera limits follow the map's size. They needed no change.
- Not measured here: a phone and the web build. The biggest map has 1.7 times the cells of before, so the fog picture and the minimap picture are 1.7 times bigger in memory. That is about 53,000 cells.

## Computer games on the new maps

Three Hard computer games of 16 minutes (tools/qa_soak_game.gd), on 0.15.0:
- Greenhold, Crown against Clockwork: both expanded (the Clockwork to 3 more Clocktowers, the Crown to 2 more Town Halls) and both attacked (the Crown at 3:20, the Clockwork at 8:00). No stuck units.
- Ashford Crossing, Clockwork against Crown: the Clockwork took 3 more Clocktowers and was winning at 16:00. The Crown expanded once.
- Twin Crowns, 2v2: everyone expanded; the Crown and Clockwork side won at 15:03.

The Crown computer sometimes drops a Town Hall it laid down because no worker came to start it (twice on Greenhold, three times on Ashford Crossing). The far sites may make this happen more often. The helper working on the computer's expansion choice may want to look at it.
