# Working Title for RTS

*(a working title: the game was called Holdfast until October 2026, but that name belongs to another game; the web address, the app file and the repository keep the old name for now so links and installs keep working)*

**Play in your browser (version 0.14.102): https://holdfastrts.github.io/Holdfast/**

**Android app: https://holdfastrts.github.io/Holdfast/Holdfast.apk** (rebuilt on every push; allow installs from your browser, then install over the old one to update)

If the game ever closes on its own, the next start shows a crash report with a COPY button: the end of the engine log, with a line for every autosave, so the moment it died can be found.

A StarCraft / Beyond All Reason style real-time strategy game for phones and the browser, made in Godot 4.7 with NES-style pixel art. Play a **skirmish** with up to eight players: 1v1, 2v2, 3v3, 4v4, you against two or three computers, or a free-for-all. Each slot picks a player (you or an Easy, Normal, Hard or **Brutal** AI), a faction, a colour and a team. If nobody is "you", the computers play and you watch. The Brutal AI micro-manages every fight and cheats (+50% income). All three factions are playable: **the Crown**, **the Brood** and **the Clockwork**.

**Online (first version):** ONLINE in the main menu hosts or joins a game by room code, 1v1 or two friends against a computer, web and Android together. It needs a free relay once: see [tools/relay/README.md](tools/relay/README.md) and [docs/multiplayer_plan.md](docs/multiplayer_plan.md).

See [GAMEPLAN.md](GAMEPLAN.md) for the design, the three planned factions and all the art prompts.

## How to play

Destroy every enemy building to win.

Each faction builds its own economy, every Town Hall, Burrow or Clocktower allows only so many economy structures, and every faction's land runs out in time (soil, saltpetre, ore, each Feeding Pit's ground), so you grow by taking and holding expansions. Each faction's economy works differently.

**Reserves:** the map holds no resources: every hall's land holds its own, invisible reserves, drawn out by the economy buildings that belong to it (each belongs to its nearest hall). A Town Hall has **9,000 gold of soil** for its Farms and **1,320 powder of saltpetre** for its Powder Mill; a Clocktower **11,400 ore** for its Ore Crushers; a Burrow (at any level) **10,500 biomass of ground** for its Feeding Pits. Spent, the soil and ground pay a third and the saltpetre and ore a quarter, so every faction must expand: a new hall brings fresh land (one raised where a hall fell takes over what was left there). Warnings come at 25% and when a reserve is spent, the match log lists what's left at each hall, and reserves are saved with the match.

**Efficiency bars:** over each of your economy buildings (Farm, Powder Mill, Feeding Pit, Clocktower, Boiler, Ore Crusher) a thin bar with a % shows how well it's working right now: **(workers on it / its full crew) x (its base's reserve: 100% fresh, falling to what spent land pays)**, so fewer workers or an older base mean a lower %. Green from 80%, then yellow, red from 40% down. An Ore Crusher has no crew of its own: its staffing is the Winders on its Clocktower and Boilers. Select the building for the breakdown, e.g. "Efficiency 53%: 5/8 workers · soil 78%".

### The Crown: dig in
- You start with a **Town Hall, one Farm and 4 Labourers** (300 gold and **no powder**): more labourers first, then a **Cottage** (it unlocks the **Barracks** and the **palisade and gates**), then the first big decision. **Two openings: Powder Mill first for Musketeers, or Horse Breeder first for a Horseman rush.** Musketeers (and Guardsmen) need a **Powder Mill**, Horsemen and Officers (and their armoured ones) a **Horse Breeder** (no Foundry needed any more); Dragoons need both.
- **Labourers** work **Farms** (up to 8 each, every hand working at the same rate). A Farm is a big square (**6x6 tiles**): the windmill in the middle, crop rows all round it (bare furrows, then green shoots after each harvest, then gold wheat), and every farmer works his own stretch of a row, hoeing and stepping along it. The fields can be walked through (by the enemy too); nothing can be built on them. A right click (or tap) with labourers anywhere on a farm, its fields too, even on the men already working them, puts them to work there. A farm ripens into a **380-gold harvest** all at once, faster with more hands (about 40 s with a full crew of 8). A fully worked base (16 farmers + 6 millers) makes about **1,140 gold and 120 powder a minute**.
- **Haulers (the Town Hall's counter):** right-click Labourers onto your **Town Hall** (the same click that sends them to a Farm; up to **6**) and they become its **haulers**: they stop farming and do only what the base asks for. A small counter over the hall reads **free/assigned** (1 hauler: `1/1`, two: `2/2`; one out on a job: `1/2`; none yet: `0/0`), and the hall's card says "Haulers 1/2". Horses are not units you command: a hauler leads them. The moment a Barracks or Foundry queues a unit that needs a horse, the request exists; when the Horse Breeder finishes the horse, the nearest free hauler fetches it, takes it by the **leash** (up to **4** horses at once; other horses waiting close by go on the same trip), walks it to the building that needs it, hitches it (it walks in: the Horseman comes out riding, the gun with its team) and goes back to the hall. Powder works the same way: a Powder Wagon on SUPPLY that has room for a keg posts a request, a hauler takes a keg from the Powder Mill and carries it to the wagon, and returns. Requests are served oldest first by the nearest free hauler and are **reserved**: two haulers never take the same horse or keg. If a trip is cut short (the hauler is killed or ordered elsewhere, which also un-assigns him; the building is gone) the leash is let go, a keg goes back into the stores, and the request is served again. A hauler is an ordinary Labourer otherwise (he can be ordered, killed, repaired). **No haulers, no stall:** with none assigned, or a ready horse that no hauler has come for in 25 s, the old way still works (a recruit fetches his own horse, a gun's horse walks over). The computer assigns 1 hauler once it has a Horse Breeder, a gun or a wagon, 2 from 6 horse-using units, 3 from 12.
- **Horse Breeder** (100 gold, 4x3 tiles: a stable and its paddock, ADVANCED page): a plain production building, not an economy one: it pays nothing, raises nothing by itself, needs no crew, and you can build **as many as you like, anywhere**. It trains horses like the Barracks trains men: **HORSE** (40 gold, 15 s, no supply), a 5-place queue, **CANCEL**, and **RALLY** to send each new horse somewhere (a Barracks or Foundry, say); there it grazes. It also unlocks Horsemen. So a unit and its horse cost about 10 gold more than the unit alone did before horses.
- **Soil fatigue:** each Town Hall's farmland holds **9,000 gold**, shared by its farms (a farm belongs to the nearest hall). Every harvest draws its gold from it; once it's spent, those farms pay **a third** (about minute 10 for a base worked hard from the start). The Town Hall and every Farm show the soil left ("Soil: 62%"), tired fields turn yellow-brown, and you're warned below 25% and when it's spent. A **new Town Hall comes with fresh soil** (one rebuilt where a hall fell takes over that tired land); new labourers go to fresh farms first.
- The **Powder Mill** (100 gold, up to 6 labourers, 0.33 powder a second each) grinds **saltpetre** into **black powder** for the bank. It draws on its Town Hall's **saltpetre**: 1,320 powder's worth, about 11 minutes of a fully worked Mill ("Saltpetre: 62%" on the Town Hall; warnings at 25% and when it's spent), then it grinds at a quarter. It is a horse mill: while labourers work it, a harnessed horse walks round the millstone's track turning the runner stone by its arm (it stands still when nobody does). Its front row is its **work yard**, walkable like a Farm's fields (nothing can be built on it): the labourers dig at the three heaps, carry the loaded shovel along the front and tip it into the hopper inside the mill's own ground, so buildings, walls or a cliff right against it never block them (with the yard's open sides all built up they go in through the shed). Like it, buildings only animate while they're in use: a Farm's sails turn while its fields are worked, a Foundry, Barracks or Assembly Works smokes while it trains, a Boiler while it fills batteries. Powder: Musketeers cost 10, Balloons 20, Powder Wagons 30, and the bank pays for every refill of soldiers' and wagons' powder.
- **Personal powder:** every **Musketeer** carries **10 shots** (**20** once refitted with the **Shot Pouch**), every **Field Cannon 8** (round shot or canister, one each) and every **Mortar 6** shells, shown on its card at the bottom ("Powder 9/10", a musketeer's reload beside it: "Reload 3.0 s", counting while he reloads). A musketeer that runs dry fights with the **bayonet** until it has 3 shots again; a dry cannon or mortar can't fire. "Out of powder!" shows over them as they run dry (once for a whole line), and every so often you're told where to take them: "Musketeers out of powder: bring them to a Mill, Barracks, Hall or Powder Wagon". They refill by themselves (2 shots a second) within **3 tiles** of your **Powder Mill, Barracks or Town Hall** (paid from the bank: 0.5 powder a musket shot, so a full musketeer costs 5 (10 with a pouch), and 1.5 a cannon shot or mortar shell; with the bank empty the refill waits) or of a **Powder Wagon** with powder left (from its store). Short fights near home never notice; long pushes need a wagon.
- **Powder Wagon** (Foundry, 70 gold + 30 powder, and a horse, supply 2, 220 hp, armour 1, cannon speed, no weapon): carries **120 shots** for the soldiers around it (it shows its unload pose while handing them out). Parked within 3 tiles of a Powder Mill, Barracks or Town Hall it fills 8 shots a second, paid from the bank at **1 powder per 2 shots** (a cannon shot takes 3 of its shots). **SUPPLY** (glows while on; **on from the start**, its button turns it off): while it's out in the field with room for a keg, the Town Hall's **haulers** (see below) fetch **30-shot kegs** from the nearest Powder Mill (15 powder each, paid from the bank when loaded; at most 2 kegs on the way to one wagon) and carry them to it, wherever it is. Nobody is pulled off a Farm or a Mill for it; with no haulers the wagon only fills at a Mill, Barracks or Town Hall. A labourer killed with a keg **blows up** (40 damage, 1.5 tiles). When the wagon is destroyed it **explodes**: up to 170 damage within about 3.5 tiles (scaled by the powder aboard, at least 70), friends included, so keep it behind the line and guarded.
- Each **Town Hall** allows 2 Farms and 1 Powder Mill within 14 tiles (Horse Breeders aren't limited). **Cottages** add supply (max 100).
- **Palisade and gates** (the kinds `wall` and `gate`; 25 and 50 gold a piece, 300 health and armour 2 each: a gate is no tougher than the palisade, it just lets your side through): lay them in a line (pick the start, then the end; your labourers build piece after piece). A gate opens for your side and your allies and stays shut to the enemy; it swings open only for men actually going through (their way runs through it, or they're right at it walking in), not for those idling beside it. Every main base sits on high ground with one ramp (two in four-player games), so a few pieces wall it off. Enemies that can't get through hack at the wall. The palisade joins up like Age of Empires 2: each piece picks its picture from which neighbours (north, east, south, west) are your palisade or gates (a straight, a corner, a T, a cross, an end or a lone post), and the pieces round it change when one is built, lost or abandoned. Gates turn to match the palisade they're placed in; R turns one by hand. A gate laid beside palisade running only north-south (or laid as part of a north-south line) faces that way, drawn side-on with its doors swinging out east and west, and the palisade joins it at its north and south posts; beside palisade running only east-west it faces that way and joins at its east and west posts. Where the palisade doesn't say (none beside it, or some both ways) it takes your last R choice while placing (ROTATE on the card; remembered). A gate still planned or being built turns when it gets its first palisade neighbour; a finished gate only turns by hand (select it, ROTATE), and then stays that way. The computer's gates follow its ramp walls (by the lie of the land where those don't say).
- **Tech step:** the Brood's and the Clockwork's ranged units need their second economy building, which they don't start with: Spitters a **Brood Pen** (5 essence an egg), Bulwarks and Arc Throwers a **Boiler** (the Clockwork starts on the Clocktower's own power). **Musketeers** need only a Barracks, but cost 10 powder each: the 80 you start with buys the first eight, then a **Powder Mill** has to grind more.
- **Observation Balloon** (Foundry, 125 gold + 20 powder, 2 supply): a pilot and a basket. The pilot doesn't fight: the balloon fights only with the **Musketeers you load into it**. It sees 14 tiles, so your cannons can fire as far as it looks, and Musketeers in the basket shoot 35% further than on the ground. Slow and fragile.
  - **Powder store:** 40 musket shots, full when it's rigged. Musketeers aboard shoot with their own powder and top it up from the store. Stopped within 2 tiles of a **Powder Wagon** (with powder), **Powder Mill**, **Barracks** or **Town Hall**, the pilot lowers a rope and hauls up a 10-shot keg (3 s; 5 powder from the bank, or taken from the wagon's store) until the store is full; moving off reels the rope in. **RESTOCK** (E) flies it to the nearest one and waits there until it's full.
  - **Dropship:** it carries up to **4 Musketeers or Labourers**. **LOAD** (L) with infantry selected along with it boards them all; otherwise press LOAD and tap the men (or right-click the balloon with them selected). They walk under it and climb in while it comes down and holds still, so it must hover over ground they can reach. Passengers stand in the basket, still count toward supply and can't be hit; carried **Musketeers fire from the basket** with their own range, damage, reload and powder (refilled from the balloon's store). **UNLOAD** (U) and tap a spot: it flies there and lets everyone off on the nearest open ground (over a cliff edge too); tap the balloon itself or **DROP HERE** (U again) to let them off where it is. If it's destroyed, everyone aboard rolls the fall: about a third die (their bodies lie where it comes down; the Brood can harvest them), the rest live bruised, hurt (yellow health) or crippled (red health); men still walking over to board are not aboard yet and live, as are men already climbing out during a drop.
- **Barracks** (only trains): **Musketeers** (A) and, once you have a Horse Breeder, **Horsemen** (D): fast light cavalry, one pistol shot then the sabre (115 gold and a horse, 140 hp, armour 1). Bayonets do +50% to them. Also **Dragoons** (F; with a Powder Mill and a Horse Breeder): one click trains a Musketeer and his horse, for the Musketeer's price (60 gold + 10 powder) and the horse's (40 gold, unless a spare one is kept for him), and he comes out on foot, gets on and rides to the rally point like a Horseman. And **Officers** (G; with a Horse Breeder): a Horseman on foot, trained without a horse for the Horseman's price less the horse's (**75 gold**, 18 s, 3 supply like a Horseman). With Armour the armoured ones join the card: Guardsman (S), Cuirassier (C), **Armoured Dragoon** (V) and **Armoured Officer** (B), each at its Armour fee on top. On the Barracks' card the musket men come first, then the cavalry, each light one followed by its armoured one (Musketeer, Guardsman, Dragoon, Armoured Dragoon, Horseman, Cuirassier, Officer, Armoured Officer), then RALLY, CANCEL and AUTO REFIT; labels too long for a button read ARM. DRAGOON / ARM. OFFICER.
- **The cavalry's pistol** (Horseman, Cuirassier, Officer, Armoured Officer): one loaded flintlock shot, **30 damage at up to 110 px** (a musket reaches 190), a single ranged blow like a musket ball (so a rider it kills may leave his horse loose; it can't hit flyers and isn't wasted on buildings). He fires it first, from the saddle or on foot (the saddle strips show him draw, aim, fire and lower it), and then fights with the **sabre** as before (18 damage every 1.1 s; the Cavalry Charge still doubles the first sabre blow after a gallop). He **can only reload on foot**: an Officer reloads by himself in **4 s** when no enemy is at his sabre's reach, spending one of the **4 charges** he carries (refilled like a Musketeer's powder, near a Mill, Barracks, Town Hall or Powder Wagon, from the powder bank); with no charges he can't reload. Recruits come out loaded; mounting and dismounting keep the pistol as it is. The unit card shows a little pistol by the health bar (bright: loaded; grey and crossed: empty) and "Pistol LOADED" / "Pistol EMPTY: ..." in its line. **HOLD FIRE** (F, a toggle on the card; glowing = on) keeps the shot: he never fires it by himself and fights with the sabre only (even on ATTACK), until you turn it off. It's kept in a saved match.
- **HOLD FIRE for every Crown gun:** Musketeers and Guardsmen, Dragoons (in the saddle and on foot), Field Cannons, Mortars and the Observation Balloon (its basket) have the same toggle (F; one button for a mixed selection). Held, they never open fire by themselves: no targets of their own, no firing back when shot at, and **attack-move doesn't fire** (a held Dragoon on attack-move rides on instead of getting down to shoot). An **ATTACK order** on a target still fires. The bayonet still fights: a held Musketeer takes on a man who comes right at him. The card says HOLD FIRE; it's kept in saves. (The balloon has no ATTACK of its own: held, its basket doesn't fire at all.) (HOLD, H, is still stand-ground.) **Foundry**: **Field Cannons** (170 gold and a horse), **Mortars**, **Observation Balloons** and **Powder Wagons**. **Church**: one **Missionary** per church. (The Pikeman is gone: the Musketeer's bayonet does his job. A saved match with Pikemen brings them back as Musketeers.)
- **Musketeers shoot or stab by themselves** (no button): at range they fire (one of their 10 shots, 20 damage); when an enemy on the ground comes within about a tile (edge to edge), or once they're out of powder, they use the **bayonet** (18 damage every ~1 s, no powder; +100% against cannons, +50% against horsemen and cuirassiers, +30% against spitters; it can't reach flyers). The musket keeps reloading while they stab. Ordered to ATTACK something farther off, they keep shooting at it while they have powder.
- **Light and armoured.** Every Crown soldier comes in two versions. The **Musketeer** and the **Horseman** are tagged *light*; refitted (or trained) with Armour they become the **Guardsman** (+1 armour, +20 health, morion and breastplate) and the **Cuirassier** (+1 armour, +30 health, 4 slower, helmet and cuirass), tagged *armored*. The kind stays the same underneath (saves, hotkeys and the AI see a Musketeer or a Cuirassier-kind horseman); the name, tag, portrait and sprites follow each soldier's armour, and so do the bonuses against him: canister does +50% to light men, round shot +75% to armoured ones.
  - Once Armour is researched the Barracks shows **both buttons** for each: **MUSKETEER (A)** and **GUARDSMAN (S)**, **HORSEMAN (D)** and **CUIRASSIER (C)**. The armoured recruit costs the Armour fee on top. Before that only the light ones show.

  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Musketeer | 60 + 10 powder (+ lock and bayonet fees) | 70 | 0 | 62 | light |
  | Guardsman | Musketeer + 12 gold + 4 powder | 90 | 1 | 62 | armored |

- **Horses heal and season:** a horse heals by itself (0.5 health a second, ridden, in harness or grazing); horses are never raised. A horse **seasons** with the ground it covers under a rider or in harness (a gun's, mortar's or wagon's team): every 100 tiles a rank, up to 3, each +15% health (a rider gets it too: a Dragoon on a fully seasoned horse has 27 more) and +1 supply's worth of essence in its carcass. It keeps its rank whoever rides it; the card shows "Seasoned horse (rank 2)". A seasoned horse's carcass (or a rider's body on one) shows its essence in pixel digits, like a veteran's body, so the Brood can see which dead horses are worth harvesting.
- **Mounted Missionary:** a Missionary can **MOUNT** a horse too: as fast as a Messenger, unarmed and as untouchable away from enemy buildings as on foot. He **gets off to bandage** (and back on after); to **raise the fallen he gets off by himself** (and stays on foot). DISMOUNT gives back the Missionary.
- **Horses.** Horses are real units, bought at a **Horse Breeder** (40 gold, 15 s each; nothing comes free) in many coats (dark, bay, grey, white, spotted). They graze where they're left, have 60 health, cost no supply and can't fight. The top bar counts yours ("Horses: 4"). Every mounted or horse-drawn unit keeps the coat of the actual horse it got.
  - **One click trains the rider and his horse:** a **Horseman / Cuirassier** (Barracks), a **Field Cannon, Mortar or Powder Wagon** (Foundry) each needs a horse. Pressing its button finds one: a horse of yours standing within **2 tiles** of the building walks in (as before; a led one counts); else a **free horse within 4 tiles of one of your Horse Breeders** is **reserved** for that queue entry; else the **nearest breeder queues one** for it, paid at once (**40 gold** on top; without the gold the button says "Not enough gold for its horse"). The top bar shows reserved ones ("Horses: 3 (1 reserved)"); nobody else can take or mount a reserved horse. A **Horseman** (or a **Dragoon**) then trains **on foot** (an Officer; a Musketeer), gets on his horse and rides to the Barracks' rally point; the horse is brought to him (see **Haulers**) and only when you have no hauler (or none came within 25 s) he walks to the breeder for it himself. A **gun's or wagon's** horse is brought to the Foundry the same way (by a hauler, or by itself when you have none); the gun comes out **when its horse is there** (its bar stays full meanwhile; a horse killed on the way is replaced: another spare, or a new one bought). **CANCEL** releases the reservation and refunds the unit; a horse the breeder was training for it is **kept** (it comes out a free spare); a horse that already walked in comes back out. Spare horses can still be trained at the breeder and kept.
  - **Teams:** a Cannon, Mortar or Powder Wagon draws **1 to 4 horses**. **HARNESS** (Q) sends one more free horse to be hitched each time, **+12% speed** for each beyond the first (4 horses: a cannon 38 -> 52, still far slower than cavalry). The extra horses are drawn hitched in pairs in front of the vehicle, each in its own coat: the second horse walks beside the vehicle's own (the wheel pair), the third leads in the middle, the fourth makes a leading pair with it; while a gun is set up they're led off with its own horse. The card says "3/4 horses +24% speed". While it has extra horses they take **30% of every blow** (front one first, 60 health each); one killed drops the speed again. Teams and each horse's coat and health are saved.
  - **LEAD** (Q): every man on foot (Labourer, Musketeer, Guardsman, Officer or Armoured Officer; and a Messenger, and the Clockwork's Winders) takes up to **3** free horses nearby by the reins: the first walks at his side, the others in a string, with a rein drawn from his hand to the bridle. A right click on a horse with **Labourers** selected does the same. **RELEASE** (W) lets them go where they stand. Horses selected by themselves walk where they're sent (right-click a Barracks: they stand by it).
  - **MOUNT** (T; or a right click on a horse with **soldiers** selected): he walks to the nearest free horse and gets on (1.5 s), the two becoming one unit. **Labourer -> Messenger** (unarmed, 124 speed, sees 9 tiles: a scout; he can still lead horses), **Musketeer -> Dragoon**, **Guardsman -> Armoured Dragoon**, **Officer -> Horseman**, **Armoured Officer -> Cuirassier**. **DISMOUNT** (T, 1 s, anywhere): the horse stands beside him, his side's, unled; if he walks on it stays there (lead it, remount it, or lose it to a thief). A **Horseman dismounts** as an **Officer** (his pistol, which he can reload on foot, and the **sabre** up close, 18 damage every 1.1 s; light), a **Cuirassier** as an **Armoured Officer** (the same, armoured). Everyone keeps his armour, locks, bayonet, pouch, health share, kills, powder, a loaded or empty pistol and veterancy both ways.
  - **Dragoons fight on foot.** Mounted they ride at horseman speed and have no attack at all. Told to **ATTACK** (or on an attack-move with an enemy in range) they **dismount (0.8 s)**, fire as their foot version (reloading **1.3x** a Musketeer's time), and **get back on by themselves (0.8 s)** when ordered to move or when the target gets away. **HOLD** keeps them on foot. Their reload only runs on foot: in the saddle it waits. Ride in, dismount, fire, mount, pull back, dismount, reload.
  - **Guns and wagons:** **RELEASE HORSE** (W) unhitches one horse of a cannon's, mortar's or wagon's team (the front one first; free for a rider). With none left it's **stationary**: a gun goes straight to its set-up pose and still fires, a wagon still supplies, but nothing rolls until you **HARNESS** (Q) one again (your nearest free horse walks over; or right-click the gun with a horse selected or led). Unlimbering keeps the horse hitched.
  - **Stealing:** a horse **left alone** (nobody of yours leading it or coming for it, none of your units within **2 tiles**) changes hands when an **enemy man** walks up to it, like sheep in Age of Empires: any Crown man on foot or riding, or a Clockwork Winder. **The Brood never takes horses; it can only kill them** (and everyone shoots a horse only when there's nothing else).
  - **Loose horses:** in a fight a rider can die while his horse lives. It depends on the blow that killed him: a **single shot** at range (muskets, pistols, Spitter and Bloater acid globs, a Stormcaller's, Ornithopter's or Arc Thrower's single zap) leaves the horse alive **40%** of the time; a **single blow** in melee (sabre, bayonet, Clockwork melee) **25%**; the **Brood's jaws and claws never** (the swarm eats the lot), nor **anything with splash or area** (round shot, canister, mortar shells, powder blasts, chain lightning, Tesla and tower zaps, storms, gas and acid clouds, Bloater bursts, the Thresher's saws, spells), nor a horse with 20% of its health or less. The horse **bolts**: it gallops 5 to 7 tiles away from whoever killed him, then calms down and grazes there, **belonging to nobody** (a greyish-white saddle cloth, "Horse (loose)" on its card, in nobody's horse count; it heals like any horse). **Any man who can lead horses, of any side** (Crown men, Clockwork Winders), takes it by walking up to it, or with LEAD, MOUNT, HARNESS or a right click on it. The **Brood can kill it** (their creatures go for one only with no real enemy in reach) and its carcass is theirs to harvest; nobody else attacks it. The rider's body is then **only the man** (on foot, worth no horse to the Brood); a Missionary can still raise him, and if his loose horse is still within 8 tiles he walks over and gets back on. A **Field Cannon or Mortar** destroyed by a shot or a blow (not a blast) gives each horse of its team the same chance; a **Powder Wagon** blows up and its horses with it. The computer's Crown sends a free man to catch loose horses in its base or by its army (with no enemy close), a Clockwork Winder close by takes one, and the Brood kills the ones beside its creatures. The match log notes each side's first horse bolted and first caught, and the totals at the end.
  - **The computer** picks an **opening** each game (written in the match log): **Powder Mill first** for a Musketeer line (the default), or **Horse Breeder first** for a **Horseman rush** (on the Rush plan, and otherwise about 3 games in 10: one Barracks, the second farm after it, the breeder buying horses from the start, no Mill, Armoury or raids until its first 3 Horsemen are out, and they ride out together); either way it builds a Cottage before its Barracks and palisade. When a horse waits at its Foundry for a gun it saves the gold for it. ### The Brood: the swarm
- **Grubs** haul food from **Feeding Pits** (a crew of **6** each, one more for every 4 tiles beyond 6 from the capital, up to 12) to the capital **Burrow**, the only place they unload. A load pays for its round trip, and the farther the pit, the more each load is worth. A pit with a full crew of six draws roughly **200-260 biomass a minute**.
- **Feeding Pit ground** (0.14.42): every pit has its **own ground, 3,000 biomass**, and the Burrows none. Each load is taken out of its pit's ground; a spent pit yields **nothing** and its grubs leave for a pit with ground left (a well-worked pit lasts about 12-15 minutes). Each pit claims a **circle of 5 tiles**, and your living pits' circles may not overlap (centres at least 10 tiles apart: 4-5 pits fit in a main). A dead pit leaves an **old ground** spot keeping what was left; a new pit over old spots takes the smallest amount left among them, so fresh ground means fresh land. While placing a pit you see every pit's circle and its ground left; a selected pit shows "Ground: %", and each pit warns when its ground runs low and when it is spent.
- Pits and every Brood structure except Burrows must grow on your swamp. **Clutch Mounds** (the supply building) spread it, and must themselves stand on swamp joined to a Burrow's (10 tiles of swamp each): plant them forward and defend them (a fallen mound's swamp shrinks back). Feeding Pits are limited only by their circles and the swamp's reach; each Burrow allows 1 Brood Pen.
- A grub **turns into** a building and is used up.
- **Harvest the fallen:** select grubs and press **HARVEST**: they stay where they are and wrap every body nearby (friend or foe) in a cocoon, carrying it to a Burrow as essence: 1 per supply of the dead unit plus 1 per kill it made (a dead horse counts as 1 supply). Bodies rot away after 2 minutes, and a grub carrying a cocoon can be caught on the way home.
- **Veterancy (all factions):** every kill of an **enemy** gives a unit +1% damage (up to +50%); its own men killed by its splash never count (they show on its card as "Friendly fire: N of ours killed", just for interest). It shows on the sprite, not over it: a **Crown soldier's** small details (the gold lace of a Musketeer's or Dragoon's tricorne, the band at the brim of a Horseman's plumed hat, the comb and brim of a Guardsman's, Cuirassier's or Armoured Dragoon's helmet) are drawn **black** under 25 kills, **silver** at 25 and **gold** at 50; a **Brood creature's skin** (the team colour on its crests stays) is as painted, then **amber-ochre** at 25 kills and **obsidian** (near black, a violet sheen) at 50. Workers, guns and the Clockwork show nothing. The selection panel shows it ("Veteran · 7 kills · +7% damage"), and a gold kill count sits in the corner of each portrait when several units are selected.

### Watching the computers play
With no "you" in the lobby the computers play and you watch, and the **VIEW** button under the clock picks how:

- **Free · all vision** (the default): you move the camera, no fog of war.
- **Free · P1 Crown vision** (one per player): you move the camera, under that player's fog of war: what its side sees and has explored, enemy buildings as it last spotted them, on the minimap too.
- **Follow · P1 Crown** (one per player): the camera drives itself to show what that computer is doing, under its fog. Every second it looks for, in order: a fight its units are in (the biggest, but it stays with the one it's watching until it has been quiet for 3 s), its army on the attack (the front of the wave), raiders at its base, a building it has just placed (a new hall a little longer), its army, its hall. It pans smoothly, with a quick fade for a long jump across the map, and holds each sight at least 3 s (6 s before leaving for a quiet look home). Zoom stays as you set it.

Moving the camera yourself while following (drag, edge or arrow pan, the minimap, a jump to a group or hall) switches to **Free** with the same player's vision, and says so; pick Follow again to resume. On a computer, **0** is all vision and **1-8** follow that player. The view you pick is kept for the next match you watch (until the game closes); it isn't saved with a match.

### Match log (for playtesting)
Every match keeps a plain-text log: who played (and at what AI level), what each side built, lost and cast, the first of each unit type, the walls laid, a snapshot of every player each minute (income, resources, supply, workers, halls, army and its value, kills and losses) and the final result with kills by unit type. **COPY LOG** on the end screen copies it to the clipboard; the last 20 logs are also saved in `user://logs`.

### Computer plans
In the skirmish lobby each computer player has a **PLAN**: Balanced, Rush (early, small, constant attacks; expands late), Economy (expands early and often, attacks late and big), Timing attack (techs up and holds everything back until about 6-7 minutes, then attacks wave after wave), Air rush (Brood only: a Roost early and masses of Skywings) or Random. A wave that dies having killed less than 60% of what it was worth makes the next one wait for 1.4 times the army, and a **Rush or Air rush that fails** stops trickling in: the computer switches to a Balanced game (workers, expansions, tech), holds everything back for 2.5 to 4 minutes (unless its army gets huge) and comes back in force, with at least 24 supply (the Brood 30, and only once its swarm is worth 80% of your army). Every plan spends its bank: more Barracks, Foundries and Assembly Works, deeper queues, and the Brood builds more Burrows (its eggs come from its halls).

### Closing the game
The match is saved every 15 seconds (and whenever the app goes to the background): if you close it, **CONTINUE** on the main menu picks it up where it was (soldiers stand where they were, workers go back to work). **MATCH LOGS** on the main menu lists every match's log, including an unfinished one, each with a COPY button.

### Workers work by themselves
**WORK** is on by default (its button glows): a worker with nothing to do goes back to a free post on its own, after building, after walking to a rally point, after a STOP. Press WORK to switch it off for the selected workers (to scout, or keep a builder free) and again to switch it back on.

### Buildings
- Every building is on the **BUILD** card (more pages if they don't fit).
- **ABANDON** a building that isn't finished: 75% of its cost comes back (all of it while it's still a ghost). **DEMOLISH** (press twice) pulls down a finished one.
- **Ghost sites (as in StarCraft 2):** a placed building is only a **ghost** until its construction actually starts: the Labourers arrive and begin (Crown), the grub morphs into it (Brood) or the crate lands (Clockwork; while it's carried or lying dropped on the way, the site is still a ghost). A ghost isn't solid (anyone walks through it), can't be attacked or targeted, gives no sight and doesn't keep you in the game; only you and your allies see it (translucent): to the enemy it's invisible, on the map, the minimap and to the computer. Your side's ghosts reserve their ground for your side's buildings, but an enemy can build over them. When construction is due to start, the footprint must be clear: your (and your allies') units standing on it are told to step out and carry on, and the worker waits; **enemy units don't move**, so a site can be blocked on purpose. Still blocked after about **5 s** (or the ground taken meanwhile), the building **fails**: all of its cost comes back, the ghost goes ("Can't build there: the spot is blocked.") and the worker goes idle or on to its next Shift-queued order. The computer places the same way and plans again when one fails (its ramp wall too). A **Brood or Clockwork ghost whose own worker is no longer coming** (the grub or Winder sent elsewhere, stopped or killed before it starts, or it can't get there: stuck with no path) **goes, with all of its cost back** (no other worker would ever take it up); a Shift-queued one stays while it's in the worker's queue, and a Clockwork crate dropped on the way keeps its site. **Crown ghosts too**: one no Labourer is coming to (he was sent elsewhere, stopped or killed, or nobody was sent) goes, all of its cost back, after about 2 s. A Labourer who finishes a site takes up the nearest unfinished one close by, so a palisade laid without Shift keeps its pieces while a Labourer is working along it; a Shift-queued site stays while it's queued, and a Crown site that has begun (any progress) is never swept (ABANDON it). Ghosts are kept in saves.
- Labourers repair buildings, Winders repair constructs and buildings (the REPAIR button, E). As in StarCraft, a full repair takes about as long as building the thing did (never slower than 20 hp a second per worker) and costs a quarter of its price: a wall piece is back in a few seconds for a couple of gold.
- **REPAIR is also an autocast** (like WORK): a tap (or E, or a click) picks a damaged building to fix; a **long press** on a phone (**right-click** on a computer) switches **automatic repair** for the selected workers (the button glows while it's on). With it on, an idle worker fixes damaged buildings within 8 tiles by itself (Winders: constructs too), and one at its post looks round every couple of seconds for a damaged building within 5 tiles; with WORK on as well, repair comes first and WORK takes it back to its post afterwards. Labourers start with it off, Winders with it on (as they always did).
- Select several buildings of the same kind (or a control group of them): training goes to whichever is least busy, and a rally point applies to all.
- Enemies walled out with no gate break the wall down.

### Line move
Drag a line and your selected units spread out evenly along it (as in Beyond All Reason): good against splash, for firing lines and for holding a choke. Computer: hold the right button and drag (a short right click is still a normal command), or press MOVE / ATTACK and drag with the left button. Phone: tap MOVE or ATTACK, then drag the line. With ATTACK they attack-move to their spots.

### Area damage hits everyone
Canister, round shot, cannon splash, mortar shells, the Steam Walker's steam, the Spore Mother's spit blobs, the Stormcaller's storm and exploding Powder Wagons and kegs hurt your own units too, so where your men stand matters. **Round shot** flies over everything to where it's aimed and then ploughs through everyone in its line, your own and allied men too (from 30 px before where it lands to 90 px past it); men under its flight, between the gun and the target, are safe. Units only fire an area attack on their own when the enemies in it clearly outweigh the friends (by supply, 2 to 1): a cannon says "Holding fire: own men where it lands", a mortar "own men in the blast", and a set-up gun picks another target without friends there (and looks past targets inside its minimum range). An explicit ATTACK order fires anyway, and **canister never holds** (it still hurts friends in its cone). A selected cannon of yours shows its **line of fire** on the ground: a faint line for the flight out to full range, and the stretch the ball ploughs as a band at its target (or, while you give an ATTACK order on a computer, at the mouse), red over your own or allied men, who'd be hit; for canister, the cone. Faint while limbered.

### Replays
Every match is recorded: a skirmish, a match between computers you watch, and online (the host records every side and sends each friend the whole replay when the match ends; a friend who leaves early keeps his own, marked YOUR VIEW ONLY). **REPLAYS** on the main menu lists them, newest first, with the result, map, length, version and players: **WATCH**, **KEEP** (never deleted by itself), **EXPORT** and **IMPORT** (a file to send a friend) and **DELETE**; the newest 30 not kept stay (Settings > REPLAYS KEPT). A replay is not a video: watching, you move the camera anywhere, select anything, play at half speed to 8x, jump on the timeline, and look through everyone's eyes or one player's fog, or follow a player's own camera. See [docs/replays.md](docs/replays.md).

### Editor: sandbox and map editor
**EDITOR** on the main menu has two tools:

- **Sandbox**: set up any match in the usual lobby, then use the SANDBOX panel in the match. You can spawn any unit or building of any faction for any player, delete things, add resources, lift the fog, switch AIs off, speed up time (x2, x4), or take **control** of another player.
- **Map editor** (on a computer or a phone):
  - **NEW** picks the size: presets from 48x48 (tiny 1v1) to 192x160 (4v4) and 192x192, or your own width and height in steps of 4, square, wide or tall. Pan and zoom as in a match (right drag, WASD and the wheel on a computer; two fingers on a phone, where **DRAG: PAINT / PAN** at the top makes one finger move the map too).
  - **Terrain**: ground (also the eraser), high ground, ramps, water (lava in volcanic and ashland lands), fords, trees and rocks, with a **round or square brush of size 1-8** or **FILL** (floods an area of the same terrain). A ramp painted across a plateau's edge is squared into a clean rectangle; one that doesn't join high ground to low ground shows **red**.
  - **LAND** and **MIX**: every biome, each with a swatch of its ground, and an optional second one blended in (diagonal, patches or middle).
  - **PROPS**: all the decorations in a palette grouped by kind (trees, rocks, plants, ruins, buildings, camp, farm, stores) with thumbnails, plus whole **groups** (farm, camp, ruins, village...) placed in one click. ERASE (or a right click) removes one. Big ones (red frame: tents, houses, arches) block like rocks; the rest are only for looks, in the editor and in the match.
  - **Spots**: 2-8 **START** spots, each with a team (**TEAM** changes it): two even teams make a 1v1 to 4v4 map, everyone alone a free-for-all, and the map records its mode. **HALL** adds expansions, **REMOVE** takes spots away.
  - **MIRROR** copies everything you paint and place, decorations too: TURN 180, LEFT | RIGHT, TOP | BOTTOM, 4-WAY, or TURN 90 on square maps, on any size.
  - **CHECK** runs the named maps' own checks (`scripts/map_check.gd`): every spot reachable on foot, each main on high ground with one ramp narrow enough to wall, no decorations on ramps or on a hall's land, room for every start's economy (farms and mills), an expansion near each start, a mirror twin for everything. Problems and warnings are listed on the right; picking one outlines it on the map and moves there. A map with problems can still be saved (with a warning); one that passes also joins the random pools of its mode.
  - **UNDO / REDO** (Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y), up to 40 steps. **HELP** explains it all.
  - Maps are saved on the device (`user://maps/`, with a picture), show up in the Skirmish map list under **your own maps** with their mode and size, e.g. "Bridges (your map, 2v2, 120x100)", with their picture in the lobby; PLAY takes you straight there. (Maps saved with the old rock deposits still load; the outcrops are ignored.)

### Maps
27 maps, chosen in Skirmish. The map list starts with six random choices: **Random 1v1 maps**, **Random 2v2 maps**, **Random 1v1+2v2 maps**, **Random 3v3 maps**, **Random 1v1+2v2+3v3 maps** and **Random all maps** (each picks, when the match starts, among its maps that hold all the players), then every map by name with its mode and size, e.g. "Ashford Crossing (1v1, 96x96)", then your own maps. Picking a map sets the players up for it (a 3v3 map: six players, three a side, keeping the first players' choices); a classic map takes up to four. If the players change afterwards (a preset, ADD PLAYER, ×) and the map no longer holds them, the choice falls back to the random pool for that many players; START stays off with a reason when nothing fits. The choice is saved with the lobby settings.

Every map is fair: it is turned through its middle, so every player has a twin spot on the other side with the same ground. Each main base sits on high ground with one ramp that can be walled, and is **all buildable**: no trees, rocks or solid decorations on its plateau (only walk-through ground detail), has a natural expansion below it, and more hall spots (high-ground mains nobody starts in are expansions too). Allies start next to each other. The named maps always look the same (their own layout and dice); the classic ones are drawn anew from each match's seed.

- **High ground:** units below can't see up a cliff unless one of their side is up there (or a flyer is overhead), and they can't shoot what they can't see.
- **Cliffs and ramps:** cliffs block walking and ramps don't. Flyers ignore both.
- **Decorations:** farms, camps, villages, ruins, shrines, wrecks, lumber yards and forges, and flowers, logs and stones strewn by biome. Small ones are only for looks (a building or wall put over one clears it); big ones (cottages, tents, towers, arches, haystacks, snowy rocks) block like rocks. None stand on ramps, fords or a hall's land.
- **Biomes:** grass, autumn, desert, snow, marsh and volcanic, and from the second tile set heath, jungle, canyon, tundra, ashland (lava), pinewood, steppe, coast, old kingdom, bog, glowwood, fall and badlands.
- **No tile seams** (`Game._render_ground`): no ground picture tiles seamlessly, so on loading a set each variant's stray edge lines (left over from the atlas grid) are mended, light or dark bands along its edges are flattened, and its brightness is evened out to the set's mean. Each cell (or 2x2 block of a 64x64 set) picks a variant, and for organic grounds a mirror, unlike the ones left of and above it. Across every cell edge of the same level (not ramps, not cliff faces against the ground) an 8-pixel band each side is mixed: each side shows the other's picture mirrored over the edge past a wavy, dithered cut, a different one per edge, so no straight line or regular pattern is left (chunks are drawn with a one-cell margin so their own edges blend the same). Borders between patches of different grounds meander along the map instead of following the cells, and a plateau's rim darkens in a smooth gradient instead of a dark cell each. Water and shallows tile cleanly among themselves and are left as drawn. It's only the picture: the map, minimap colours and lobby previews are unchanged, and the map editor uses the same painter.

### Computer controls
- **Left click** selects; **Shift**-click adds or removes; **Ctrl**-click or double-click selects every unit of that type on screen. **Left drag** box-selects (Shift adds).
- **Right click** commands: move or attack, put workers on a Farm, Mill, Pit or Boiler, help build, or set a building's rally point. Right-click on the minimap sends units there.
- **Building:** the ghost follows the mouse. Click to place, **Shift**-click to place another (queued on the same workers), right click or **Esc** cancels.
- **Looking at the enemy (as in StarCraft 2):** click an enemy (or loose) unit or building to read its card; a drag box with none of yours in it picks one enemy unit (the one nearest its middle), else one of their buildings. You can never order them.
- **Rally on a unit (as in StarCraft 2):** set a building's rally on one of your units and the rally point follows him; the soldiers it trains walk after him and keep a couple of steps behind until he falls.
- **PATROL (P):** pick a place: the selected soldiers walk there and back again and again (an attack-move each way), fighting whatever comes on the way, until given another order. Shift queues it.
- **Who units go for (as in StarCraft 2):** units on their own (idle, on HOLD, on an attack-move or patrol) pick enemy **fighters** first, then other units (workers, Missionaries, unarmed riders, wagons), then **buildings** (economy first); walls and gates only when nothing else is in reach or they're in the way. A unit hitting a building on an attack-move looks again every half second and **turns on an enemy unit that comes in reach**.
- **Right-click on the ground (computer) is a plain move**, as in StarCraft 2: units go where they are told and don't stop to fight on the way, even under fire (A, then click, to attack-move). On a phone a tap on the ground stays an attack-move.
- **Group moves bunch up (as in StarCraft and Beyond All Reason):** no pre-made formation. A group that is already together keeps its shape and moves as one blob; a group spread out closes into a clump at the point, each man keeping his side of it.
- **Shift-queued orders (as in StarCraft):** hold **Shift** while you give an order and it goes after the units' current orders instead of replacing them. This works for a right click (move, attack a target, work at a Farm, Mill, Pit or Boiler, help build, repair), **MOVE** and **ATTACK** clicks or line drags, **REPAIR** and placing buildings and walls. Each unit keeps up to 16 orders and takes the next when the current one is done (it arrives, its target dies, its building is finished). MOVE, ATTACK, REPAIR and placement stay open while Shift is held and close when you let it go. Shift-placed buildings are built one after another in click order: a Labourer builds each in turn, a Winder takes each crate in turn, and a Shift-placed wall is built piece by piece. A grub becomes its building, so each Shift-placed Brood site takes a selected grub that doesn't already have one. A plain order (or STOP or HOLD) clears the queue. Working at a post never ends, so it is always the last order. Your selected units show their queue: a faint line through each waypoint with small markers (green move, red attack, yellow build or work). Queues aren't kept in a saved match (units come back without one). On a phone there's no Shift, and taps work as before.
- **Hotkeys:** every command-card button shows its letter. Hover for cost and details.
- **Tooltips:** rest the mouse on a command button (a third of a second) for its name, cost in both resources, time, supply, what's still missing (in red), its hotkey and a line or two about it; on a unit or building in the world, your own or the enemy's (1.5 s), for its name, health and what it does; on a resource, supply or quick button (under a second). They sit by the cursor, stay on screen, linger a moment after the mouse leaves, and never take a click. Crown and Brood for now (Clockwork units and buildings have none yet). **Settings > Tooltips** switches them off. **B** build menu (then the building's letter, e.g. **F** Farm), **A** attack, **M** move, **S** stop, **H** hold, **G** work, **D** deploy or pack, **R** rally, **X** back or cancel, **N** more.
- **Hotkey bar** (above the selection card, as wide as the card is): **F1** IDLE (every idle worker), **F2** ARMY, **F3** MAKE (every building that makes something, the Town Hall too: train from all of them at once), then groups **1 2 3 4 5 6 7 8 9 0**. **1–9, 0** select a group (twice: jump to it), **Ctrl+number** sets the group to the selection, **Ctrl+Shift+number** adds the selection to it, **Shift+number** adds the group to your selection (as in StarCraft 2; to drop a unit, select the group, Shift+click the unit off and press Ctrl+number again). The same with the mouse on the buttons (Shift, Ctrl or Ctrl+Shift + click). Peeking out above the bar are nine camera tabs **F4–F10**: **F4–F10** jump to a saved view (position and zoom), **Shift+F4–F10** (Ctrl works too) save the current view. Groups and views are kept when you leave a match and CONTINUE, and in the autosave. In a browser, Ctrl+1–8 and F5 may be taken by the browser (use the buttons, or Shift+click). **Space** jumps to the selection. **Tab** flips a command card's pages (the MORE button).
- **Camera:** mouse wheel zooms; pan with the screen edges, the arrow keys or a middle-button drag.
- **Cursor:** the mouse pointer shows what a click does: a brass pointer (a green ring at its tip over your own units and buildings), the red crosshair in ATTACK (and for spells, LOAD, UNLOAD, OVERLOAD), the blade ring over an enemy your selection can attack (in ATTACK, or with soldiers selected, where a right click attacks), the green arrow in MOVE, the pointer with gold dots in PATROL, the hammer when building or repairing (a barred red circle where the building can't go) and the pennant for rally points. Computer only (`scripts/cursors.gd`).
- **Watching computers:** **0** all vision, **1-4** follow that player (see *Watching the computers play*).

## Art pipeline

The source sheets from ChatGPT live in `art/`. `python3 tools/import_art.py` turns them into game sprites in `assets/`:

- removes the painted checkerboard (or uses real transparency),
- finds every sprite, groups them into rows (animations) and the 5 direction panels, splits frames drawn touching,
- shrinks them to the true NES pixel size,
- snaps the red team-colour parts to two exact key reds, which `shaders/team.gdshader` swaps for each player's colour,
- aligns every frame at the feet and writes `scripts/art_data.gd`.

To add a unit: put its sheet in `art/crown/`, add it to `UNITS` in the script with its row names, and run it.

`tools/rebuild_art.sh` runs the whole pipeline in order. After the general importer come passes that replace or repair parts of its output:

- `python3 tools/import_cannon2.py` builds the field cannon (horse-towed, unlimbered, firing, reloading, limbering, wreck) from the three new cannon sheets, picking the good frames and fixing the rest.
- `python3 tools/import_lightning.py` cuts the lightning effects.
- `python3 tools/import_cursors.py` cuts the nine mouse cursors from `art/ui/cursors.png` (3x3 on magenta; the magenta and its anti-aliased fringe keyed out, edge pixels de-fringed), box-filtered at one common scale to `assets/ui/cursors/<name>.png` (32 px; `_48` for high-DPI desktop screens), and writes their hotspots to `scripts/cursor_hotspots.gd`.

## Project layout

| Path | What |
| --- | --- |
| `scripts/game.gd` | The match: map, A* pathfinding, separation, combat helpers, economy, fog of war, selection and commands |
| `scripts/unit.gd` | Units: orders (move, attack-move, attack, hold, gather, build), combat by type, animation |
| `scripts/building.gd` | Buildings (construction, production queue, damage states, reserves and efficiency) |
| `scripts/ai.gd` | Computer opponent (Easy / Normal / Hard) |
| `scripts/controls.gd`, `scripts/ui/hud.gd` | Touch and mouse input; resource bar, minimap, selection panel, command card |
| `scripts/spectator.gd` | Watching computers play: whose fog you see and the follow camera's director |
| `scripts/defs.gd` | Unit and building stats: tweak balance here |
| `scripts/mapgen.gd`, `scripts/map_data.gd` | Map generation, biomes and decorations; the named maps as data |
| `tools/simulate.gd` | AI-vs-AI test: `godot --headless --fixed-fps 30 --script res://tools/simulate.gd -- crown brood [map or pool] [2v2/3v3/4v4] [minutes:N]` |
| `tools/make_map_previews.gd` | The lobby's map pictures (`assets/maps`): `godot --headless --script res://tools/make_map_previews.gd` |
